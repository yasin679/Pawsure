# Pawsure: brand and product brief for social media content

Both sit beside this file. Then ask, for example: *“Using the Pawsure brief, write a 2-week Instagram and LinkedIn calendar with captions, image suggestions from the illustration library, and alt text.”* Everything an assistant needs to stay on-brand and accurate is below. **Section 2 lists the facts it may and may not claim. ## 1. Pawsure in one minute

- **Name:** Pawsure
- **Tagline:** *Be sure, whoever’s caring* (no full stop when used as a line on its own)
- **What it is:** a mobile app that shows pet parents honest evidence that planned pet care actually happened at home today, and helps them find trusted cover when their usual helper can’t come.
- **One-line pitch:** Other apps help you book care. Pawsure assures it happened, and has a backup when it doesn’t.
- **Positioning line:** Other apps sell you a caretaker. Pawsure verifies the one you already have.
- **Category:** pet-care assurance. It brings visibility, accountability and continuity of care.
- **Platform:** a mobile app for the owner. Helpers use WhatsApp (or a free missed call on a basic phone), with nothing to install.
- **Where:** a pilot is planned in Mumbai (Andheri–Powai): 20 homes, 4 weeks.
- **Built by:** Group-G, IITR (Apurva, Nikita, Neha, Rakesh, Sukanta, Tanvi, Vibhor, Yasin).
- **Stage:** working demo; pilot not yet run. Never describe Pawsure as launched or as having users, reviews or results.

## 2. Facts: what content may and may not say

**Safe to say**
- Pawsure works with the helper you already have: your maid, a family member or a walker.
- Your helper needs no app. She gets reminders and checks in on WhatsApp, in her own language (Hindi, Marathi or English in the demo), or with a free missed call.
- She sends a photo of the task (the bowl, the walk, the medicine). Never her face.
- Each task gets an honest status: **Confirmed**, **Likely** or **Not confirmed**. Pawsure never says “failed”.
- You review her photo and approve the task in one tap.
- When your help can’t come, **Pawly**, the AI assistant, finds your own friends nearby who are free. **Nothing is sent until you approve.**
- Alerts come only when something slips.
- The research figures in section 4, always with their source wording (“from our primary research and survey of 45 pet parents”).

**Never say or imply**
- That Pawsure guarantees a pet’s safety, that a visit is “100% verified”, or that pets can’t come to harm.
- That Pawsure is a vet, gives medical advice or is an emergency service. (“Pawsure is a coordination and evidence tool. It is not a vet or an emergency service.”)
- That it uses cameras, GPS tracking of the helper, or face photos.
- That it books or pays carers, day cares or walkers. Not at launch.
- That it has launched, has a number of users, ratings, testimonials or partners. None exist yet.
- That the helper is being watched or policed. The tone toward helpers is respect and credit, never suspicion.
- Invented statistics. Use only the numbers in section 4.

## 3. The problem

Working pet parents leave their dog alone for 2 to 8 hours and rely on a helper or neighbour. There are three gaps every working day:

1. **No shared plan.** Instructions are scattered across WhatsApp chats and memory.
2. **No signal.** Nothing tells you a task was done, or that a visit was missed.
3. **No backup.** When the helper can’t come, there is no plan B.

The story hook we use: *It’s 1:07 PM. Do you know if your dog has eaten?* The owner texts the helper, waits, and two hours later gets “haan didi sab done 👍”. Done when? Done what?

Cameras watch but can’t act. Booking apps confirm a booking, not that care happened.

## 4. Research (use exactly as written)

Source wording: **“From our primary research and survey of 45 pet parents (exploratory).”**

| Finding | Number |
|---|---|
| Leave their pet alone at least sometimes | **80%** (36 of 45) |
| Have changed their plans because of their pet | **69%** (31 of 45) |
| Rely on someone else to check on their pet | **62%** (28 of 45) |
| Use a pet camera | **42%** (19 of 45) |
| Have paid for pet care | **40%** (18 of 45) |
| Came home to find something wrong | **16%** (7 of 45) |

Good pairing: *42% use a camera. A camera can watch. It can’t refill a bowl.*

## 5. Who it’s for (personas, illustrative)

- **Neha, 34. Primary owner.** Out 8–10 hours a day. Her helper Sunita feeds Bruno, her dog, at lunch. Her worry: *“Is Bruno okay?”* She needs one honest status for the day and alerts only when something slips.
- **Sunita, 45. The helper.** Works across three flats. Uses WhatsApp and voice notes in Hindi or Marathi, sometimes a basic phone. Her worry: *“Will I be blamed?”* She needs a simple request in her language, no app, fair credit, and the right to stop any time.
- **Rohan and Ankita. New to the city.** No family or friends nearby. They need a trusted care circle in their own society.

**Who it is not for:** homes where someone is always in; cats with self-feeders.

The emotional jobs: feel calm and connected, no guilt about being away, trust without micromanaging, be a responsible pet parent.

## 6. How it works (the first day)

1. **Sign up with Pawly, your personal AI assistant.** A short chat: your name, your home, your pet, what they eat and when. Each answer becomes one of today’s tasks.
2. **Invite your helper.** She gets a WhatsApp message in her language, sends a photo of her ID (only the Pawsure team sees it), and joins with one tap.
3. **She gets a reminder at task time.** For example: “🐾 Coco, ⏰ Breakfast (8:00 AM). When done, send a photo or a short video of the bowl. No faces.”
4. **She sends a photo of the task.** The photo appears on the task on your Home, and the status changes to **Approve**.
5. **You approve in one tap.** The task moves to Completed. “Approved: Breakfast. Thank you, Lata!”

**When help can’t come:** ask Pawly in plain words (“Can someone feed Bruno at 6 PM?”). Pawly suggests your own friends nearby who are free, nearest first, and drafts the WhatsApp message. You approve every message.

**Launch features (P0 only; don’t mention later phases):**
- Pet profile and daily routine, set once by chat (with vet, allergies, medicines and an emergency card).
- Helper check-ins on WhatsApp or a free missed call.
- Proof of care with smart alerts: Confirmed, Likely or Not confirmed per task.
- AI Friend Finder for cover.
- **Not at launch:** hardware, mandatory cameras, cash payouts, vet diagnosis.

**Task types and their proof photo:** meal (the food bowl), water (the fresh water bowl), walk (the dog on its lead), medicine (the tablet strip), pee pad (the clean pad), litter (the clean tray), other (the pet).

| pet parent, owner | master |
| proof of care, check-in | surveillance, monitoring, tracking |
| Confirmed / Likely / Not confirmed | done / failed |

| Home (the app’s main screen) | dashboard |

Spelling: **Pawsure** (one word, capital P only). **Pawly** (P-a-w-l-y, said like “Polly”).

## 8. Brand voice

- **Warm, calm, honest.** We reassure without overpromising. “Likely” is a feature: we tell you how sure we are.
- **Plain and short.** Everyday words, short sentences, one idea per line. Write the way a kind, sensible neighbour talks.
- **Respectful to helpers.** Helpers are partners who get credit, never suspects. Celebrate them.
- **Indian and specific.** Mumbai societies, lifts, WhatsApp groups, didi and tai, chai breaks, monsoon walks. Hinglish touches are welcome in moderation (“sab done 👍”).
- **Light humour, never at the pet’s or the helper’s expense.**
- **Headlines without full stops.** Sentence case. No ALL CAPS shouting.
- **Emoji:** a few, purposeful: 🐾 ⏰ 📷 ✅ 🐶 🐱 💛. Not a row of them.

**Sample lines**
- Be sure, whoever’s caring
- It’s 1:07 PM. Do you know if your dog has eaten?
- Proof, not promises
- The bowl, never the face
- Your helper needs no app. Just WhatsApp
- Check it. One tap to approve
- When help can’t come, ask friends
- A camera can watch. It can’t refill a bowl

**Logo:** a dark-green heart with a white paw print, next to the wordmark “Pawsure” in a rounded bold typeface. Two versions: horizontal (`pawsure-logo.png`) and stacked/vertical (`pawsure-logo-vertical.png`). Keep it on light backgrounds; don’t recolour or stretch it.

Characters are round and smiley, and many wear a mustard-yellow hoodie. People appear only as illustrated avatars, never as photos. Task “proof” scenes show a hand at most, never a face.

## 11. Content pillars (ideas for posts)

1. **The 1:07 PM feeling.** Relatable moments of worry at work: the unanswered text, the meeting you can’t leave, the empty bowl at 7 PM.
2. **Proof, not promises.** How a bowl photo plus an honest status beats “sab done 👍”. Explain Confirmed / Likely / Not confirmed.
3. **Helpers are heroes.** Respect and credit for the people who do the care. No app for them, their language, never their face, the right to stop any time.
4. **Pawly, your AI assistant.** Sign up by chat; find a friend for cover in plain words; nothing sent without your OK.
5. **Research says.** One number per post from section 4, with the source line.
6. **Society life.** Care circles, neighbours helping neighbours, the Sunday paw parade, monsoon-walk tips.
7. **Pet care tips.** Leaving-home checklists, water on hot days, routine ideas. General tips only: never medical advice.
8. **Behind the build.** Design decisions (why WhatsApp, why “Likely”, what we cut) and the team.

**Formats that fit:** carousels (problem → insight → how Pawsure works), short reels (a WhatsApp chat playing out, then the Pawsure version), polls (“Have you ever come home to an empty water bowl?”), memes in the illustration style, LinkedIn posts on the design story.

**Calls to action:** “Join the Mumbai pilot” (Andheri–Powai), “Tag a friend who checks on their dog at lunch”, “Share this with your society group”.

**Hashtags (pick 3–5):** #Pawsure #BeSure #PetParents #PetParentsOfIndia #MumbaiPets #DogsOfMumbai #PetCare #WorkingPetParents #ProofOfCare

## 12. Go-to-market ideas we’re exploring (not yet tested)

- **Let the helpers sell it.** One helper works in three homes; every helper we onboard brings Pawsure into the next flat.
- **One society at a time.** An invite in the residents’ WhatsApp group, a QR code in every lift, a Sunday paw parade.
- **A care card worth sharing.** A weekly card for WhatsApp status, for example “Bruno’s week: 31 of 31 tasks covered”.
- **Offices and vets.** A pet-parent perk with offices in Powai; an emergency card at partner vets.

Growth path: 20 homes in one Mumbai cluster → 3–5 nearby societies → Mumbai-wide. Plan: Year 1 Mumbai (~1,000 users), Year 2 four cities, Year 3 eight cities. Present these as plans, never results.

## 13. Accessibility for every post

- Write alt text for every image (what it shows, in one sentence).
- Keep text on images short and high-contrast (deep green or charcoal on cream).
- Add captions to every video.
- Don’t rely on colour alone to show a status; name it (Confirmed, Likely, Not confirmed).
